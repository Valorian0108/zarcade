# The Merchant's Window, explained

A plain guide to everything in cabinet four. Every character, every prop, every
scene, in order. Read this once and the game should make sense start to finish.

## The premise

You run a small shop. The day is done. One customer ordered a custom piece and
paid a deposit into your shop account. Your supplier holds the materials
needed to build that piece. He will not release them until someone can see
that the deposit really arrived. Tonight he sent a man to check. You hold the
only key that can show the money. The game is one question: how much of your
record do you show him.

## The characters

### You, the shopkeeper
You never speak. You only press buttons and pick one card. Everything on
screen happens to you or waits for you.

### The auditor
He is the only voice in the game. He is not a government official. He is not a
regulator, not tax, not police, not compliance. He works for your supplier.
His job is to come back with proof that the deposit landed. He has no power
to take anything. Every fact he leaves with is one you chose to give. The
word "auditor" here just means a man who checks books for someone else.

### Your supplier
He never appears on screen. He holds the materials for the custom order. The
folded letter the auditor carries is from him. It is his written ask: show me
the deposit landed, or no materials. Older builds of the game called him your
partner. The screen now says supplier everywhere, so the word can never be
misread again.

### The customer
The person who ordered the custom piece and paid the deposit. Also never on
screen. One of the lessons of the cabinet is about this person: even a key
that reads your whole incoming history never says who paid. The customer's
identity stays sealed.

## The props, one by one

### The lamp
The light over the counter. Pure mood, plus one signal: the light widens when
your record board opens. Narrow light means sealed. Wide light means read.

### The slip on the counter
Your paper copy of the custom order. It carries the order reference, INV-204.
Its use is matching: when you see a payment on the record board with the same
reference in its message, that payment is the deposit for this order. The slip
is how you know which row matters tonight.

### The brass key
An incoming viewing key for the shop account. This is a real thing on the real
chain, not a game invention. What it can do: show every payment that arrived
at this one account, with the amount, your address, the message the payer
attached, the time, and the block. What it can never do: name who paid, show
money going out, show your full balance, or spend a single coin. Reading and
spending are two separate secrets. This key holds only the reading one.

### The record board
Your account's incoming side, hung on the wall like a shop notice board. The
rows are real testnet payments read live from the chain at page load. Nothing
on it is invented. The keyhole in the panel is where you turn the key to open
the rows.

### The till
The cash box under the counter. It holds money you can spend. It is not for
confirming payments. It has exactly one job in the story: in scene four you
try the same key in it and it does not turn. That is not a broken lock. That
is the whole safety lesson of the cabinet, shown instead of told. A key that
cannot open your own cash box cannot open it for anyone else either.

### The supplier's window
A small lit window on the far wall. It represents what the other side can
see. It stays dark through most of the game. Your choice in scene five
decides how it ends: all rows lit, one row lit, or dark.

### The folded letter
In the auditor's hands all night. It is the supplier's written ask for proof
of the deposit. It is a letter, not an envelope with money. It is his reason
for standing in your shop.

### The curtain
The entry screen with the setup text. One press opens it and the night begins.

## The scenes, in order

### 1. Curtain
The setup. Who you are, what the order is, what the key is. Opens on one
press.

### 2. The request
The auditor arrives and states his business. He names the deposit, the
reference INV-204, and the condition: no proof, no materials. Your button is
"Take the key". The key lifts from the counter into your hand.

### 3. The preview
You turn the key in the record board and the rows warm one by one. These are
the real payments. You see the deposit: one thousand zatoshis, reference
INV-204, at block 4543955, with another payment sitting beside it. Both came
into this one account. The lesson line: this is what the key shows, and it
never shows who paid. The button is "Try the till".

### 4. The boundary
The same key goes into your own till. It wiggles. It does not turn. The
auditor says the line you asked about: "It does not turn. That is not a
broken lock. That key can read your money. It cannot spend it." Meaning: the
lock is fine, the key is fine, and the key simply holds no spending secret.
No one holding it can ever move a coin. The button "Decide" appears after the
key stops moving.

### 5. The choice
The actual decision of the game. Three cards on the counter:

**Hand over the key.** You give him the viewing key itself. He can then read
every payment that arrives in this account, forever. The deposit is proved and
so is everything else, past and future. Sharing it is permanent. Even so, he
still cannot spend.

**Prove one payment.** You give no key. You hand over a sealed proof of the
one deposit. He checks it against the chain: amount, address, message, block.
It matches. He leaves able to prove that single payment and knowing nothing
else about you. Single payment proofs are a real feature of the chain.

**Hand over nothing.** He leaves with your word only. No amount, no block, no
proof. Your privacy stays total. The cost lands on trust: the supplier has
nothing to check, and may not release the materials on it.

None of the three is scored. None is wrong. Each leaves a different thing
open. That is the point.

### 6. The consequence
The supplier's window shows what your choice really revealed. Key: every row
lit, all your incoming history visible to the other side. Note: one row lit,
the deposit alone, checked against the chain. Nothing: the window stays dark.
The auditor says one plain line about what he now holds. Button: "Continue".

### 7. The balance
One last question before he goes: "How much is left in the account?" The
honest answer, and the game gives it: not from this key alone. The key sees
money arriving, never money leaving, so a full balance is outside its reach.
That is a limit of the key, not a trick on you. Button: "Finish".

### 8. The end card
"What your choice left open." One line naming your outcome. The three
boundaries that hold no matter what you picked: the key never names who paid,
the key can never spend, the key shows one account's incoming side only. Then
your keepsake: the real deposit, with amount, reference, transaction, block,
and a link that opens the real transaction on the public explorer so you can
check the shop yourself. Two buttons: play again, or back to the curtain.

## Quick answers to the questions that came up

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| What is the till for? | One scene: proving the key cannot spend. It is the cash box, not a payment checker. |
| Is the auditor government? | No. He is your supplier's agent. No state, no compliance, no power to take. |
| What does the key read? | Every incoming payment to this one account: amount, address, message, time, block. |
| What does the key never read? | Who paid, money going out, your full balance. It can never spend. |
| What am I picking? | How much proof the auditor leaves with: the whole key, one proved payment, or nothing. |
| Why hand over nothing? | To keep every fact private. The price is that the other side has nothing to check. |
| What is the letter? | The supplier's written ask for proof of the deposit. The auditor carries it. |
| What is the slip? | Your copy of the custom order. Its reference INV-204 matches the deposit's message. |

## What is real in this cabinet

The record board rows are real testnet payments, read live from the chain.
The deposit with reference INV-204 is a real transaction. The explorer link on
the end card opens the real thing. Incoming viewing keys are a real feature.
Single payment proofs are a real feature. The shop, the auditor and the story
are fiction built to show those real parts at work.
